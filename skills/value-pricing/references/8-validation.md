# Validation and handover

Pricing and packaging conclusions remain hypotheses until supported by evidence appropriate to the decision. Plan a validation method that fits the market, offer, customer access, and operational constraints.

## Choose the validation approach

Possible approaches include follow-up interviews, a properly recruited survey, a price or package experiment, a pilot, or analysis of observed purchases and renewals. State what each can and cannot establish. Do not prescribe a universal sample size or claim statistical precision without a design and sample-size rationale.

Before collecting data, define the target population, recruitment, consent and lawful basis as applicable, privacy controls, retention, primary outcomes, exclusions, analysis, stopping rule, and decision criteria. Pilot instruments for comprehension. Fix decision rules before reviewing outcomes when confirmatory interpretation matters.

Do not send invitations, alter live prices, or contact customers without the user's explicit authorization. Keep contact lists and identifiable responses out of the skill workspace and model prompts.

## Record decisions and gaps

Record open questions, recommended next steps, evidence required, decision owner, and status in `DECISIONS.md`. Revisit key assumptions when observed behavior differs from stated intent or modeled outcomes.

## Handover

Provide a concise report with the decision studied, configured business and pricing basis, methods selected and why, sources and dates, findings and uncertainty, assumptions and limitations, unresolved gaps, and the validation plan. Include a price or structure hypothesis only when evidence supports it. Do not imply ratification or operational readiness without an explicit decision and a feasibility review.
