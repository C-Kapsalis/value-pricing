# Structured perspective review (optional)

Use a structured review when multiple buyer, commercial, operational, or behavioral perspectives can expose assumptions the quantitative work may miss. A panel of simulated perspectives is a brainstorming aid, not independent research or a source of empirical willingness-to-pay estimates.

## Prepare a grounded brief

Summarize the decision, offering, relevant buyers, evidence, candidate structures, assumptions, gaps, and constraints. Distinguish evidence from judgment. Avoid personal records and confidential details unless the user has explicitly approved their processing and the method permits it.

Perspective cards should reflect user-provided or sourced context. Do not invent market facts or imply a generated persona represents a real segment. Include only perspectives relevant to the decision.

## Review questions

- Which buyer outcomes and alternatives matter most?
- What could prevent adoption, realization, delivery, or payment?
- Which proposed units, terms, boundaries, or bundles could seem confusing or unfair?
- Which assumptions have the largest effect on the decision?
- What evidence would confirm or challenge each hypothesis?

Record agreements, disagreements, and unsupported judgments separately. If model-generated perspectives are used, label them as simulated and do not treat their consensus as validation.
