# Buyer research and simulated panels (optional)

Choose a research instrument to match the question and the decision. Qualitative interviews can explore language, context, and alternatives. Price sensitivity questions, Gabor-Granger, or choice-based conjoint may help quantify stated responses when the population, offer, design, and sample are appropriate. No method guarantees prediction of actual purchasing behavior.

Simulated buyers can help pilot questions, identify confusing terms, and generate hypotheses. They are not a substitute for human respondents or observed behavior. Label all such outputs as simulated, avoid reporting them as empirical findings, and do not use them as demand forecasts.

## Design considerations

Before fielding a human study, define the target population, unit of analysis, recruitment, instrument, sample-size rationale, stopping rule, exclusions, primary outcome, analysis, uncertainty, data handling, and validation use. Use a pre-registration when confirmatory interpretation matters. Match prices and attributes to the actual currency, billing basis, scope, and terms under study.

Conjoint is appropriate only when buyers make meaningful trade-offs among attributes that can be presented realistically. Keep the design manageable and test comprehension. Price sensitivity instruments require credible ranges and neutral framing. Interviews are not representative estimates unless recruited and analyzed for that purpose.

The supplied panel scripts implement a particular conjoint workflow and expect a configured study schema. They are optional tools; use only with relevant inputs and a suitable design. Do not run a simulated panel or send prompts to an external model without reviewing privacy, provider, and cost implications.

## Interpretation

Report who was studied, how they were recruited, what they saw, exclusions, uncertainty, and generalization limits. Separate stated preference from actual behavior. Calibrate simulated outputs against relevant human evidence if available; calibration does not remove all bias or make simulated results representative.
